# Go / Pivot / Kill — Decision Framework
*The hardest skill in entrepreneurship is knowing when to stop.*

---

## When to Use This

Use this framework at the end of each validation cycle (every 2-3 weeks) or whenever your team is stuck debating whether to continue, change direction, or kill the idea.

This is the Google X "Astro Teller" method: **try to kill your idea as fast as possible.** The ideas that survive the killing are the ones worth building.

---

## The Decision Matrix

Answer each question honestly. Score each as Yes (✓) or No (✗).

### PROBLEM SIGNALS
| # | Question | ✓/✗ |
|---|----------|-----|
| 1 | Have 5+ non-friends described this problem unprompted? | |
| 2 | Are people currently spending time OR money on workarounds? | |
| 3 | Does the problem occur at least weekly for your target user? | |

### SOLUTION SIGNALS
| # | Question | ✓/✗ |
|---|----------|-----|
| 4 | Have users engaged with your prototype/landing page without prompting? | |
| 5 | Can you explain why your approach is 10x better (not 10% better)? | |
| 6 | Is there a reason this solution is possible NOW that wasn't possible 2 years ago? | |

### TRACTION SIGNALS
| # | Question | ✓/✗ |
|---|----------|-----|
| 7 | Has anyone given you money, a pre-order, or a signed LOI? | |
| 8 | Are users coming back without you pushing them? | |
| 9 | Is at least one metric growing week-over-week? | |

---

## Reading the Score

**7-9 ✓ → GO.** You have real signal. Double down. Stop exploring, start executing. Your biggest risk is now speed — someone else sees this too.

**4-6 ✓ → PIVOT.** Something is working, but not everything. Identify WHICH questions are ✗ and change your approach to those specifically:
- Problem ✗ → You're solving the wrong problem. Go back to discovery.
- Solution ✗ → Right problem, wrong approach. Rethink your 10x insight.
- Traction ✗ → Right product, wrong channel or audience. Change WHO you're selling to or HOW.

**0-3 ✓ → KILL.** This is not working. Killing an idea is not failure — it's the fastest path to finding one that works. The best founders kill fast.

---

## The Pivot Menu

If your score says PIVOT, pick one:

| Pivot Type | What Changes | What Stays | Example |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|---------|
| **Customer Pivot** | Who you sell to | The product | Slack: gaming → enterprise |
| **Problem Pivot** | Which problem you solve | The customer | Instagram: location check-in → photo sharing |
| **Solution Pivot** | How you solve it | The problem | YouTube: video dating → video sharing |
| **Channel Pivot** | How you reach users | Everything else | — |
| **Revenue Pivot** | How you make money | The product + user | — |

**Rule: Only pivot ONE thing at a time.** If you change everything, you're not pivoting — you're starting over.

---

## The Kill Ritual

When you kill an idea, write down:

1. **What we learned** (not "it didn't work" — what SPECIFICALLY didn't work)
2. **What surprised us** (the most interesting unexpected finding)
3. **What we'd tell someone starting this idea** (in 3 sentences)

This turns a "failure" into an asset. The best next idea often comes from insight #2.

---

## Pre-Mortem: Do This BEFORE You Start

Before running your next experiment, write:

> "It's 3 weeks from now. This experiment failed completely. What went wrong?"

List the top 3 reasons. Then design your experiment to test for those reasons FIRST. This is Astro Teller's core method at X (Google): seek the fatal flaw before investing in the vision.
