# The Mom Test — Cheatsheet
*Based on Rob Fitzpatrick's "The Mom Test" · themomtest.com*

---

## The One Rule

**Talk about their life, not your idea.** People will lie to you — not because they're bad, but because they're polite. Your job is to make it impossible for them to lie.

---

## The 5 Rules

### 1. Talk about their life, not your idea
- Bad: "Would you use an app that does X?"
- Good: "How do you currently handle X? Walk me through the last time."

### 2. Ask about specifics in the past, not hypotheticals about the future
- Bad: "Would you pay $10/month for this?"
- Good: "What did you spend on solving this last month? Show me the receipt."

### 3. Talk less, listen more
- If you're talking more than 30% of the time, you're pitching, not learning.

### 4. Seek commitment and advancement
- Words are cheap. Ask for something: time, money, reputation, or access.
- "Can I follow you while you do this next Tuesday?" > "Sounds interesting!"

### 5. Push for specifics on compliments and fluff
- When they say "That's a great idea!" → ask: "When was the last time you tried to solve this?"
- When they say "I'd definitely use that!" → ask: "Can you show me your current workaround?"

---

## The Conversation Flow

```
1. CONTEXT    → "What does your day look like when you deal with [problem area]?"
2. PAIN       → "What's the hardest part about that?"
3. FREQUENCY  → "How often does this come up?"
4. STAKES     → "What happens when it goes wrong?"
5. CURRENT    → "What do you do about it now? How much does that cost you?"
6. COMMITMENT → "Would you be willing to [specific next step] this week?"
```

---

## Red Flags (You're Doing It Wrong)

| You hear... | It means... |
|-------------|-------------|
| "That's a great idea!" | They're being polite. You pitched instead of asked. |
| "I would definitely use that" | Future tense = fantasy. Ask about the past. |
| "I'd pay $X for that" | Hypothetical money isn't real. Ask for a pre-order. |
| Everyone says yes | Your questions are leading. Flip the script. |
| No one mentions the problem unprompted | The problem might not be real. |

---

## Green Flags (You're Learning Something Real)

| You observe... | It means... |
|----------------|-------------|
| They describe specific workarounds | The problem is real and painful enough to act on |
| They've already spent money on alternatives | Willingness to pay is proven |
| They lean forward, get emotional, share details | You've hit a nerve — dig deeper |
| They offer to introduce you to others with the problem | Social proof + distribution channel |
| They ask when they can use it | Pull > Push |

---

## After Each Conversation

Write down exactly 3 things:

1. **What did I learn that I didn't know before?**
2. **What assumption was confirmed or destroyed?**
3. **What's the next riskiest assumption I need to test?**

If you can't answer these, the conversation was wasted.
