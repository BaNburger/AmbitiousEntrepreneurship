## The bar

| | Floor | Full bar |
|---|---|---|
| Conversations per student, in your shared problem space | 5 | 10 |
| Team agreement, confirmed by every founder after your end-of-week feedback round | Yes | Yes |

A talk counts once held, named and quoted in your log, for whoever led it, each person once per team this week. Friends, family and classmates don't, their intros do. Switched problem space? Earlier talks still count. No team yet? Talks with possible co-founders count until you commit, classmates included.

## How to do it

1. **Answer the full [Co-Founder Matchmaking Assessment](../../resources/Co-Founder_Matchmaking_Assessment.xlsx) alone,** then talk through every gap.
2. **Use the week as your trial.** Fit shows under load, not over coffee. Hold at least two talks with a co-founder listening, then swap.
    - Watch out. Two builders and nobody selling? Give selling an owner by your midweek day, or recruit one.
3. **Find your people.** Start with Module 00 contacts and their intros, people you see daily and where the work happens. Review sites and AI deep research show where they complain.
4. **Stay in the problem.** Ask about the last time it happened and what it cost. Don't pitch. Log every problem, unranked.
5. **Record with the consent sentence** in [Legal Basics](../../resources/LEGAL_BASICS.md).
6. **Get a blind second opinion.** Give an AI your raw notes, minus personal data and your favourite idea. Ask which problems came up most. Note where it disagrees.
    - Watch out. AI personas are rehearsal and never count.
7. **Hold your feedback round** at the end of the week. Situation, behaviour, impact, the change you ask for. Receive it without defending. Then update and confirm the agreement.
    - Watch out. Directness differs by culture. Ask how your co-founder likes feedback.

## Midweek check

10 a week is 2 a day. Behind? Change who you ask or where, not just how many. A dealbreaker came up? Talk now. Can't confirm? Part as friends while it is cheap. Your talks still count, and anyone still alone gets matched.

## Keep your records

It all lives in your [Student Toolkit](../../resources/Student_Toolkit.xlsx).

- **Team sheet.** Your skill bars and agreement, one line per topic, each founder's confirmation dated.
- **Interview Log.** One row per talk, right after it, with who led it, an exact quote, today's cost and the recording.
- **Read-out.** One slide from these, roles not names. Facts, Numbers, Quotes, Images, then what you did, learned and need.

## Pre-read for Module 02

Read the [Flixbus case](../02-finding-ideas/M02_Case_Flixbus.md) and prepare these questions.

1. **(M02-LG1)** The three friends saw a chance in a promised change of the law. Write that chance as a problem someone has. Use one sentence. Do not name a product, a bus or a platform. Say who has the problem. List what the case tells you about those people. Mark each fact as heard from a person, taken from a document or assumed by the three.
2. **(M02-LG2, M02-LG3)** Engert later named three paths. Finish the doctorates and go back into consulting. Stay in the secure jobs. Or make the jump. Score the idea of starting in this market on desirability, viability and feasibility. Use only evidence from the case. Mark each score as in question 1. Then write three sentences on why this team should make the jump now. Add the strongest argument against it, as a traveller, a fellow founder or an investor would make it.
3. **(M02-LG4)** Apart from the three themselves, no traveller speaks in the case. Pick two people the three could ask. For each person, write three questions about the last long trip they made. The questions must not mention the idea. Say which answer should make the three stay in their jobs.
