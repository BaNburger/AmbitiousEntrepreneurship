# Module 01 — Team & Foundations

Updated: 2026-10-30.

> **Core question:** Why do the best teams beat the best ideas?

**Session flow:** Input session (about 90′, with check-in, recap and read-out, lecture, wrap-up and homework) · Workshop (90′) · Case discussion (about 90′, after 4 to 6 hours of preparation at home). Each block can run on its own.

## Learning goals

1. **M01-LG1.** Students can map each founder's skills and appetite across tech, product and sales, show what their team can execute, and decide how to close the gap that would stop them from building or selling.
2. **M01-LG2.** Students can test co-founder fit before they commit, by comparing honest answers on time, money, roles and the problem space each of them wants to spend years on, and write down what they agreed, including who decides what and how a founder leaves.
3. **M01-LG3.** Students can give a co-founder feedback that names the situation, the behaviour, its impact and the change they ask for, receive feedback without defending, and adjust both to the other person's culture.
4. **M01-LG4.** Students can explain why a startup optimises growth in customers over time while a small business optimises profit, and decide as a team, with reasons, which one they are building and how far they want to take it.

**This week's goal.** By the start of Module 02, your team has a written agreement on roles, time, money, ambition and one shared problem space, tested in a week of conversations, so selling has an owner and the team survives its first fight.

**This week's bar:** Hold 10 conversations per student in your team's shared problem space, each with a named person and one verbatim quote, and end the week with a team agreement every founder confirmed after your end-of-week feedback round. The learning floor is 5 conversations per student and the confirmed agreement. Anti-fake rules: [Grading & Revenue Ladder](../../resources/GRADING_RUBRIC.md).
