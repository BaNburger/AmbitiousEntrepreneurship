# Module 03 — The Problem Space

Updated: 2026-10-19.

> **Core question:** Is this a real problem — or just an interesting one?

**Session flow:** Input session (about 90′, with check-in, recap and read-out, lecture, wrap-up and homework) · Workshop (90′) · Case discussion (about 90′, after 4 to 6 hours of preparation at home). Each block can run on its own.

## Learning goals

1. **M03-LG1.** Students can describe their problem space (who has it, their goal, the obstacle and the boundary conditions) without naming a solution, and trace a symptom back to its root cause.
2. **M03-LG2.** Students can write the job their segment hires a solution for as one sentence, together with today's workaround and what it costs in euros or hours.
3. **M03-LG3.** Students can hold a problem conversation that yields past behaviour, spend and verbatim quotes instead of opinions and compliments, and ends with a concrete next step.
4. **M03-LG4.** Students can compare segments by frequency, intensity, current spend and who holds the budget, and commit to their hair-on-fire segment on what they heard, not what they assumed.

**This week's goal.** By the start of Module 04, your team knows whether your problem is expensive enough to fix and for whom, and has named people in that segment who agreed to a next step, your first prospects.

**This week's bar:** Hold 15 problem conversations per student in your hair-on-fire segment and come back with 5 named people per student who agreed to a next step. The learning floor is 5 conversations and 2 next steps per student. Anti-fake rules: [Grading & Revenue Ladder](../../resources/GRADING_RUBRIC.md).
