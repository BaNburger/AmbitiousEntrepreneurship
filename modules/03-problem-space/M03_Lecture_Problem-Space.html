Transfer question · M03-LG1

Which problem should the repair shop investigate?

Local bike shops miss promised pickup times while mechanics wait for spare parts. Riders need their bikes for work. The shops have little cash to tie up in stock.

B. Keep the repair deadline and the cash limit in view.

Parts delays stand between the shop and its promise to riders. Limited cash stops the shop from stocking everything.

  1. AStock alerts choose a remedy before the evidence establishes that missing alerts cause the delay.
  2. BThis keeps the shop's repair promise and cash constraint without prescribing the solution.
  3. CThe stated bottleneck is waiting for parts, rather than the speed of the mechanics' repair work.
  4. DTemporary transport addresses an adjacent rider need instead of the shop's evidenced repair problem.

What would change this? If riders buy temporary transport even when repairs run on time, investigate their separate travel need.

Transfer question · M03-LG1

Which interval should they compare first to test the approval explanation?

The shop diagnoses jobs by 9 am, yet some orders miss the supplier's noon dispatch. Owner approval is required. Mechanics blame that step. The team will compare late and on-time jobs using recorded timestamps.

C. Compare the approval wait on late and on-time jobs.

If late jobs wait for approval across noon while on-time jobs receive it earlier, that supports investigating approval as the source of the missed dispatch.

  1. ADiagnosis is already complete by 9 am. Its duration does not measure the approval wait after that point.
  2. BEnd-to-end repair time measures the symptom without locating the suspected delay.
  3. CThis isolates the suspected approval wait and shows whether it spans the dispatch cutoff on late jobs.
  4. DThe supplier's dispatch interval tests a downstream explanation, rather than the mechanics' claim about owner approval.

What would change this? If the supplier received the late orders before noon, follow the delay into dispatch and delivery instead.

Ask why. Then ask why not Fix the root, not the symptom

Transfer question · M03-LG2

Which job statement fits the activity that took two hours yesterday?

After each market day, a stallholder spends her evening matching cash and card totals so she can restock. She shows that yesterday’s reconciliation took two hours. A team proposes automatic receipts.

C. Keep the restocking outcome and the observed reconciliation cost.

The job states the situation, motivation and outcome. The measured workaround is the baseline a possible solution must improve.

  1. AAutomatic receipts prescribe the team's feature rather than the outcome the stallholder needs.
  2. BFewer card payments introduce a remedy she did not ask for and omit why she needs the totals.
  3. CReconciled takings support restocking. The two hours measure yesterday's reconciliation, not a general weekly cost.

What would change this? If most of the evening is spent chasing missing stock instead, measure that separate workaround before attributing its hours to reconciliation.

Transfer question · M03-LG3

What should the interviewer ask next?

A campsite owner says arrival days are chaotic. The interviewer suspects guests need a check-in app, but does not know what happened on the most recent busy day.

A. Ask for the event before supplying its explanation.

A past event reveals who did what and where the difficulty arose. Leading with check-in pushes the owner toward the interviewer’s preferred cause.

  1. AThe owner can place the difficulty anywhere in the sequence of actual events.
  2. BThis asks about the past but presumes paperwork caused the delay.
  3. CThe proposed app puts a remedy in the owner's mouth and asks for a prediction.

What would change this? If the owner describes a clear late-payment problem instead of check-in, follow that account rather than forcing the app hypothesis.

Ask about the last time, not in general Don't lead. Discover

Transfer question · M03-LG3

Which role should the record assign to the manager?

A restaurant manager says, “I stay after closing to fix the rota.” The manager offers an introduction to the owner who approves staffing spend. The record will keep the quote and leave the unreported duration and cost blank.

B. Record the manager as problem holder and pursue the owner introduction.

The person doing the workaround need not control spending. Keep the manager's words and the evidence gaps, then make the offered buyer introduction concrete.

  1. AThe interview identifies the owner as the spending decision-maker. It establishes no manager budget.
  2. BThe manager experiences rota rework and can introduce the owner who approves spending.
  3. CThe manager described a workaround, not a requirement for automation or advocacy for a product.

What would change this? If the manager confirms delegated budget authority, record that evidence and arrange the next step with the manager.

Transfer question · M03-LG4

Which segment deserves the next focused test?

A team has two interview sets. Studios report daily booking clashes, paid overtime and an owner who controls the budget. Clubs praise the idea but cannot name a recent clash or buyer.

C. Start with the segment whose behaviour supports the painful problem.

Praise does not establish urgent demand. Daily disruption, current spend and a known budget holder give the team a defensible segment to test.

  1. AApproval of an idea is weaker than accounts of a costly recurring event.
  2. BCombining segments hides differences in urgency and purchasing authority.
  3. CThis choice follows the observed criteria rather than the size of the audience.

What would change this? If studio overtime is trivial and owners refuse any remedy, revisit severity rather than treating daily frequency alone as urgency.

The 5 golden questions, live

2STEP 2 · 35 MIN

THE 5 GOLDEN QUESTIONS, LIVE

Is the problem expensive enough to fix? Do NOT pitch.

  • 10 min: open on their day, let them raise it first
  • Last time? Hardest part? Why ×5? Solved how? What don't you love?
  • End with a concrete ask
  • 25 min: one real conversation per team, logged
