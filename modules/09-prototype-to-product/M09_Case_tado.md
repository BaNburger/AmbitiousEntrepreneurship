# tado°: Box, Service or Saving?

In mid-July 2013, Christian Deilmann, co-founder and chief executive of tado° GmbH in Munich, had sold a smart heating control at one price for eight months.[^horizon] Since its launch on 14 November 2012, tado° had charged €99 a year for the service, paid a year in advance, and had given the hardware away.[^launch] [^faq1211] On tado°'s own pages the free box had always been an offer for a time, first for the first 1,000 customers, then, as the FAQ said from April 2013, "currently free (otherwise €149 one-off)".[^launch] [^faq1304] Deilmann ran the company as one of three managing directors, with his co-founder Johannes Schwarz and with Leopold von Bismarck.[^lp4]

In June 2013 three new pages had appeared on tado.com. Two of them showed packages the shop did not sell. "Flex" cost €149 for the hardware set plus a monthly fee, €2.99 on one page and €4.99 on the other, cancellable monthly. "Life" cost €299 for the hardware set and lifetime use. The third page showed €99 a year with the hardware set included. On 19 July the page with Flex at €2.99 and Life was still online.[^lp4] [^lp5] [^lp6] [^lp4jul]

After the first year, tado°'s offer page said, a customer could cancel "the intelligent control" at monthly notice, while control by app and web stayed. The FAQ added that the hardware then belonged to the customer. tado° said its control saved a household 27% of its heating costs on average.[^offer0303] [^faq1304] How should tado° ask a household to pay for a box whose automatic control ran on a paid service?

## What Heating Cost

The Bundesweiter Heizspiegel 2012, the national heating cost survey published on 22 May 2012 by the non-profit co2online, put the cost of heating a 70-square-metre flat in 2011 at €890 with oil, €715 with gas and €785 with district heating.[^heizspiegel] tado° used a figure of its own. Its offer page of March 2013 said a typical household in Germany spent about €954 a year on heating and that tado° saved 27% on average, "in this case a saving of €257". The page gave no source for the €954. From March to June 2013 the offer pages put the saving at "about €285 a year".[^offer0303] [^offer0316] [^offer0616] The 27% came from tado°'s beta test in the winter of 2011 to 2012, in which, according to the launch release, customers saved between 12% and 40% against their heating before tado°.[^launch] Exhibit 3 sets the figures side by side.

Nest, the smart thermostat a German home automation blog compared tado° with in February 2013, was still sold only in the United States. The blog presented tado° as "an alternative for the German-speaking market".[^hc] RWE sold a SmartHome system in which the central unit cost €189 and a starter pack with two radiator thermostats €319. Mobile access was included for two years and cost €14.95 a year after that, a technology site reported in February 2013 (Exhibit 3).[^rwe]

## A Box and an App

Deilmann, Schwarz and Valentin Sawadski founded tado° in Munich in 2011.[^launch] Two founder networks in Munich, the LMU Entrepreneurship Center and UnternehmerTUM, gave them free offices, Deilmann recalled in 2016.[^ms2016] Two venture capital investors, Target Partners and Shortcut Ventures, backed the company before its launch.[^year]

The product was a small box that replaced the wall thermostat or was attached directly to the boiler, and a gateway that linked it to the home's internet router.[^hc] [^faq1304] An app on the residents' smartphones detected when the last person left home and turned the heating down, and warmed the home again as soon as someone set off back. tado° also took weather forecasts into its control.[^hc] [^launch] Systems had run in homes since February 2011. In a closed beta, by Deilmann's account in 2012, tado° connected 50 homes, and all its users together had saved more than 100,000 kilowatt hours of energy.[^eco12] [^year]

Most customers installed the box themselves. "Currently, 95% of our customers do it themselves and it takes them around 10 to 20 minutes," Deilmann said in January 2013.[^hy] tado° did not work everywhere. "In some houses and flats tado° is not yet available at the moment," the offer page said in March 2013, and some heating systems needed an extra sensor at €49 one-off. Visitors found out by continuing into the order process, which began with a check of their heating system.[^offer0303] [^order0131] The shop's terms let customers pay by direct debit, credit card, cash on delivery, prepayment, giropay, Sofortüberweisung or PayPal.[^lp4]

Before the launch Deilmann also spoke of partners. In a talk in 2012 he said tado° had partnered with "several multipliers which will bring us to the masses", telecom companies, utilities and heating technology firms. "Those companies often have large customer bases already sell subscription models or have network connections into their homes," he said.[^eco12] In March 2013 tado° and the Munich green energy supplier Polarstern announced that both would offer their existing customers an "exclusive promotional offer".[^polarstern] The same month tado° released its app in English and opened a closed beta in other European countries, half of whose installations it planned in the United Kingdom.[^tnw]

## €99 a Year

tado° launched on 14 November 2012 in Germany, Austria and Switzerland.[^year] The launch release set a market launch price of €99 a year for using tado°, a price "far below the annual savings that tado° can achieve", and promised the box free to the first 1,000 customers. A customer who decided after the first year that tado° was not right for them could cancel monthly and get the rest of the year's fee back pro rata.[^launch] The shop priced the hardware at €149 one-off and showed it at "€0 instead of €149". It called tado° "a service with an annual fee", and the FAQ called the payment a "simple pre-paid model".[^product1211] [^faq1211]

Deilmann explained the model in a video interview recorded at the LeWeb conference after the launch and posted in February 2013. The host suggested that the only sticking point might be an expensive box. At the moment, Deilmann answered, tado° had designed the product "as a service", "because we work for the customer continuously". It kept developing the product, he said. "We charge an annual fee of €99," cancellable monthly, and "we give the customer the hardware so that the whole thing is possible."[^venturetv]

The offer page raised the question itself. "Yes, tado° is a subscription. But with us that is nothing bad at all." Why a yearly fee? tado° "works for you around the clock", the page said, runs computing capacity for every user, buys weather forecasts from third parties and keeps improving its software and apps. "The reduction in your heating costs will usually exceed the cost of tado° many times over."[^offer0303]

The terms were not worded the same way everywhere. The FAQ said the contract ran for one year first, then renewed with monthly notice. The order page of January 2013 sold "tado° for one year (cancellable monthly)", and in June the test page with the yearly price said "cancellable monthly" without a first term.[^faq1303] [^order0131] [^lp6] After the first year, the FAQ said in April 2013, "the hardware is then also your property", and the basic functions, manual control, stayed available.[^faq1304]

The free box changed its framing over the months. Until at least 8 March 2013 the FAQ limited it to the first 1,000 customers.[^faq1303] On 3 March the offer page gave the hardware set, "worth €149", to customers in the beta phase as a thank-you, and from mid-March an image on the page called it "our market launch offer for beta customers".[^offer0303] [^offer0315] By 8 April the FAQ said the box was "currently still free (actually €149 one-off)". From mid-March tado° also offered a one-year money-back guarantee. A customer who stopped using tado° in the first year got the full amount back on returning the hardware.[^offer0315] [^faq1304] Exhibit 1 shows the offer as customers saw it from November 2012 to June 2013.

## What Customers and Advisers Said

In January 2013 Deilmann pitched tado° at the hy! conference to a panel of three, who gave feedback on stage. He said tado° had launched its web shop "a couple of weeks ago".[^hy] Two of the panelists had a Nest at home. The first said that a consumer sale "might actually be better if you went straight to the utilities and get them to implement it". The second agreed and offered what the panelist called "the other way". "I would price it based on how much it lowers the use of energy and somehow figure a cost sharing with the user." No share was named. The third called tado°'s yearly pricing "a big minus for consumers down the road", once they worked out its cost over time against a "200 euro box right from the get-go that they own forever". "Paying for something, unless it's got something really magical on the back end as a service, it's going to be challenging." The first panelist came back to price. "If you price at $99, you also have to tell people that you're saving $1,000 a year or something of that sort."[^hy] Most of the feedback concerned other points, such as the box having no display of its own, the number of parts in the box and homes where several people shared the heating. Deilmann answered the point on the display. No answer of his on pricing is recorded.[^hy]

In late March 2013 the iPhone news site iphone-ticker.de wrote about tado°, and readers argued about the fee in the comments. "ANNUAL FEE??? That's worth a one-off €99 … no more!" one wrote. Another had ordered. "€99 with a one-year money-back guarantee is risk-free!" A third found €99 a year too expensive. "€99 for 3 years would have been OK. I'd spend €250 at most on a solution like this," the reader wrote, and "tado should definitely offer a buy option too!"[^ticker] tado° answered three other comments in the thread, on homes with several residents, on central heating and on data protection. The thread as captured shows no tado° reply to a comment on price (Exhibit 4).[^ticker]

tado° sold online, and it also offered a phone call. A landing page of April 2013 for visitors from Facebook ads gave "all information on availability and ordering" by phone, or a free call-back to anyone who left a name and number.[^lp2] In June a form on the offer page asked visitors a question. "Not sure yet? Tell us what information you are missing or what we can improve."[^offer0616] A job advertisement captured on 27 June 2013 sought a team lead to build up direct sales in Germany, winning new customers "on site, by phone, at trade fairs".[^job]

tado° did not publish how many customers it had, how many cancelled, how many used the money-back guarantee or what a box cost it to make. On 31 January 2013 its order page showed 1,252 Facebook likes and 280 Twitter followers.[^order0131]

## Three Test Pages

On 18 and 19 June 2013 the Internet Archive captured three new pages on tado.com, named LP4, LP5 and LP6. They shared one layout and the same product claims, and their offers differed (Exhibit 2).[^lp4] [^lp5] [^lp6] LP4 showed two packages, "Flex" at €149 for the hardware set plus €2.99 a month, cancellable monthly, and "Life" at €299 for the hardware set plus lifetime use. LP5 showed the same two packages with Flex at €4.99 a month. LP6 showed one package, €99 a year for using tado°, the hardware set included, cancellable monthly.

None of the pages took an order. Each asked for an e-mail address in exchange for more information, and LP4 and LP5 then asked which package the visitor was interested in. In the page code the button on all three pages sent an event labelled "pricingtest" to the company's web analytics, and LP4 carried Google Analytics experiment code, as the offer page had in March.[^lp4] [^lp5] [^lp6] [^offer0315] tado° did not publish how many people saw each page, how many left an address or which package they chose. On 19 July 2013 LP4 was still online, unchanged.[^lp4jul]

For a customer the offers added up differently over time. In the first year Flex cost €184.88 or €208.88, Life €299, and the subscription €99 with the box free or €248 at its standard terms with the box at €149. Over three years Flex cost €256.64 or €328.64, Life still €299, and the subscription €297 with the box free or €446 at its standard terms (Exhibit 2).

## Mid-July 2013

In mid-July 2013 the FAQ had said since April that the box was "currently free", and on 19 July LP4 still showed Flex and Life to whoever reached it.[^faq1304] [^lp4jul] Four ways of charging were on tado°'s pages or had been put to Deilmann. There was the subscription, as sold since the launch with the box free or at its standard terms with the box at €149. There were Flex and Life from the test pages, and there was one panelist's idea of a price tied to the energy saved. Each would decide what a household paid for, the box, the service or the saving.

How should tado° ask a household to pay?
