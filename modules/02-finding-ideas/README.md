# Module 02 — Finding Ideas

Updated: 2026-10-19.

> **Core question:** Where do startup ideas actually come from?

**Session flow:** Input session (about 90′, with check-in, recap and read-out, lecture, wrap-up and homework) · Workshop (90′) · Case discussion (about 90′, after 4 to 6 hours of preparation at home). Each block can run on its own.

## Learning goals

1. **M02-LG1.** Students can find idea candidates in places where they see problems early, such as their own itch, their expertise or technology, a shift in their field or something that is missing, and write each as a problem someone has, not as a product.
2. **M02-LG2.** Students can generate many ideas alone before anyone judges them, sharpen the strongest from general to specific, and cut to one with a desirability, viability and feasibility gate scored on evidence they hold, every score marked heard or assumed.
3. **M02-LG3.** Students can say in one to three sentences why their team should solve this problem now, from their unique insight, why the problem is worth solving and what this team can execute, and defend it when critics attack.
4. **M02-LG4.** Students can test whether their chosen idea's problem is real in short conversations that ask about the last time it happened instead of pitching, and change the idea on what they heard.

**This week's goal.** By the start of Module 03, your team has picked one idea, knows why you are the team for its problem, and has heard named people describe the last time it hit them, the first names on your prospect list.

**This week's bar:** Hold 15 problem conversations per student about the problem behind your chosen idea. The learning floor is 5 conversations per student. Anti-fake rules: [Grading & Revenue Ladder](../../resources/GRADING_RUBRIC.md).
