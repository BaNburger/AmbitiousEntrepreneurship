# Fairphone: 5,000 Phones by 15 June

Updated: 2026-10-19.

> Module 04 · Validating Problems · Case, read before class

Case for Module 04, Validating Problems, of Ambitious Entrepreneurship. Prepared by Bastian Burger from public sources as a basis for class discussion. The case does not judge whether any decision was right or wrong. Licensed under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 except third-party material as marked.
{: .byline }

On Monday 13 May 2013, Fairphone, a start-up of seven people in Amsterdam, adopted the terms and conditions of the online shop in which it planned to sell its first smartphone.[^terms] [^tc] The phone did not exist as a product yet. A factory in China had been chosen and had agreed to a model that let the sales start with only a small down payment, and the first production run was planned for that year.[^tc] [^ballester] The terms set a "sales benchmark" of 5,000 phones "which must be sold before the 15th of June 2013". If the benchmark was missed, Fairphone had the right to terminate the contracts, and a buyer whose contract was terminated would get back the money already paid.[^terms]

The price had been set at around €325 since April, and Fairphone had promised the people on its newsletter list "the first opportunity, before anyone else, to buy".[^mier0404] On 10 May the list counted more than 14,000 subscribers.[^tc] The pre-sale itself had not opened.[^tshirt]

Bas van Abel, who had founded Fairphone, led the team.[^tc] [^qa1] In front of it lay one question. Should Fairphone open its pre-sale on this benchmark, asking for €325 for a phone that would ship in the autumn, from people who so far had signed up for a newsletter, with the right to cancel every order if fewer than 5,000 phones were sold within about a month?

## From a Campaign to a Company

Fairphone had started in 2010 as a campaign. It aimed, in van Abel's words of May 2013, at "creating more awareness around the abuses in the supply chain of electronics". The team then decided to make a phone of its own, to expose every step of production, with an emphasis on the mining situation in the Democratic Republic of the Congo.[^qa1]

Until the company was set up, the project was funded mainly by Waag Society, an Amsterdam institute where it was embedded, with some support from the Dutch government and social funds such as Stichting DOEN. Van Abel recalled in May 2013 that operators had been willing to support the mission and the sales, and that "an increasing amount of people" had been asking when the phone would hit the market, so they could buy it. Because Fairphone was going to sell an actual product, the team set up a company, a social enterprise, with seed money from an investor it knew from the Waag incubator. "There is a difference between seed money to get you going and cash flow investments to do the sourcing. We denied cash flow investments," van Abel said.[^qa1]

By 10 May 2013, according to the Dutch trade service Telecompaper, which summarised a report in the Financial Times, Fairphone had developed a concept smartphone with the support of the London incubator Bethnal Green Ventures and €400,000 from a private investor. The phone was a dual-SIM handset designed to be easy to repair and recycle, and it was to go into production that year in a first run of 20,000. It would be sold mainly through the website, but also through operators. Vodafone and KPN supported Fairphone, and KPN would also offer the phone, Telecompaper wrote.[^tc] Fairphone's homepage listed Waag Society, Schrijf-Schrijf, ActionAid, Stichting Doen, Vodafone, Bethnal Green Ventures, KPN and others as supporters.[^home]

## A Factory in China

In March 2013 Fairphone was searching for a production partner and answering questions its community had sent "through e-mail, Facebook, and Twitter".[^partner0328] Production in Europe proved not feasible, and the team chose A'Hong, a manufacturer in China.[^tc] Miquel Ballester described the choice in a blog post that Fairphone's homepage linked to on 13 May. A'Hong offered a one-stop shop and a tested design, and, he wrote, "who would have guessed, they would agree to our crowdfunding model? Making it possible for us to start the sales with only a small down payment."[^ballester]

The terms gave one reason for the benchmark of 5,000 phones, "In order to finance and continue the fair production of the Products".[^terms] Nothing published before the terms of 13 May says who at Fairphone set the number, how it was worked out or when it was first stated in public. The benchmark equalled a quarter of the planned first run of 20,000 phones (Exhibit 3).

## Fourteen Thousand Sign-ups

The call to action on Fairphone's homepage read, on 13 May, "Get it? Join the group of [number] subscribers for the first FairPhone." A counter filled in the number.[^home] The counter recorded the size of the newsletter list. It stood at 4,670 at the end of January 2013 and at 12,840 on 8 May (Exhibit 1).[^counter]

On 4 April Joe Mier, Fairphone's community manager, wrote that the list stood at 9,367 the evening before and 9,523 as he posted. "Sometimes when we get a sudden rise in subscribers it comes as a happy surprise," he wrote, "like this time when I only found out we were over 9,300 from a write-up in the Austrian Der Standard yesterday." He wanted everyone who wanted to buy a phone to register now, "or we can't guarantee one for you later", and asked them to share Fairphone's story on Twitter and Facebook. "To reach more subscribers, and ultimately buyers, we need our community's help in spreading the word."[^mier0404]

The same post promised the community of "almost TEN THOUSAND" the first opportunity to buy the phone.[^mier0404] On 29 April Mier wrote that Fairphone was "gearing up for our pre-orders period in May – with a huge blast of events and buzz in the media".[^tshirt]

What Fairphone did not publish was how many people visited the website, how many subscribers opened its emails, or how many had said they would buy at €325.

## The Offer and the Benchmark

The terms adopted on 13 May laid down the benchmark and what followed from missing it (Exhibit 2). If 5,000 phones were not sold before 15 June, Fairphone had "the right to terminate the contract", and buyers whose contract was terminated were "entitled to a full refund of the money the Customer already has paid".[^terms] The terms also said that Fairphone would do its utmost to deliver within six months of ordering, and that all dates were "always indicative".[^terms]

The offer itself had been public since April. The 4 April post scheduled pre-sales for May and shipping for the autumn. The phone would be unlocked and sold "exclusively through our website", at a price "currently set around 325 euros".[^mier0404] Telecompaper gave the price as 325 euros on 10 May.[^tc] The shop had been built by then. Its product feed dates the phone "excl. charger" to 26 April and the charger, sold separately, to 29 April.[^rss] The refund in the terms covered "the money the Customer already has paid", which assumes that buyers paid before the benchmark date.[^terms] What 5,000 phones meant in money, in production and in subscribers is set out in Exhibit 3.

## Why Ask Buyers to Pay First

Van Abel explained in May 2013 that independence was what made Fairphone credible. Crowdfunding the investment needed to go into production fitted that, he said. "This way our buyers not only buy the phone, but actually make Fairphone possible."[^qa2]

## 13 May 2013

The terms were adopted. More than 14,000 people had signed up to hear about the first Fairphone, and the pre-sale had not opened. Opening the pre-sale meant asking those subscribers first, and later anyone else, to pay €325 upfront for a phone they could not hold before the autumn. If fewer than 5,000 phones were sold before 15 June, Fairphone had the right to cancel the orders, and buyers would get back what they had paid.

Whether to open the pre-sale on this benchmark was the question in front of van Abel's team.

## Exhibit 2: The Sales Benchmark in the Shop's Terms, Adopted 13 May 2013

> **4.3** In order to finance and continue the fair production of the Products, Fairphone has a sales benchmark of 5,000 (five thousand) mobile phones, which must be sold before the 15th of June 2013. If the sales benchmark number of 5,000 phones is not met at the 15th of June 2013 at the latest, Fairphone has the right to terminate the contract at first instance and without having to pay any damages or compensation, whatsoever.
>
> **4.4** In case of termination of the contract due to circumstances as stated in aforementioned article 4.3., the Customer will be entitled to a full refund of the money the Customer already has paid to Fairphone. To the refund payment no interest shall be due.

Source: Fairphone B.V., Customer Terms and Conditions, Fairphone Shop, articles 4.3 and 4.4, version adopted on 13 May 2013, see the notes.
