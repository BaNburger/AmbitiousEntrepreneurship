# Module 12 — Fundraising & Demo Day

Updated: 2026-10-26.

> **Core question:** Can you tell the story — and show the money?

**Session flow:** Input session (about 90′, with check-in, recap and read-out, lecture, wrap-up and homework) · Case discussion (about 90′, after 4 to 6 hours of preparation at home) · Demo Day (in place of the workshop block, every team pitches, ends the course). Each block can run on its own.

## Learning goals

1. **M12-LG1.** Students can explain why venture capital funds only companies that could return a whole fund, the power law, and judge how big their own venture can get, designing it for customers beyond their home market from day one, naming the second market where their problem costs most, and launching one market at a time while home carries the first revenue.
2. **M12-LG2.** Students can decide whether, when and from whom to raise, read from burn, runway and growth whether their venture is default alive or default dead and from lifetime value, acquisition cost and payback whether its growth pays, and choose the money that fits the company they want to build, from customers' money and grants as non-dilutive money to angels and venture capital raised from strength, counting a patent as a moat once customers exist.
3. **M12-LG3.** Students can pitch their venture in five minutes in Sequoia's order, open with the problem as customers described it, give the "why now" from their hypothesis about the world in five years, prove it with verified revenue and the one number that matters most, and answer an investor's or a customer's questions with honest numbers.
4. **M12-LG4.** Students can decide after Demo Day, on the evidence of the whole course, whether their company goes on, pivots or stops, and if it goes on, agree in writing who does what with how much time, the next revenue target and the next kill line, settle who owns the code, the brand and the money, book the next outside meeting and keep their accountability group meeting.

**This week's goal.** By Demo Day, your team pitches its venture on proof, not promises, knows how big it can get and which money fits, and leaves with a decision on the company, a plan and a group that keeps you selling.

**This week's bar:** Reach €10,000 cumulative revenue from outside your social graph by Demo Day, rehearse your pitch with three outsiders who can repeat your problem and your one number, and within seven days of Demo Day write your verdict on the company with what it needs. The learning floor is the Demo Day minimum of €2,000 verified with your evidence pack, one such outsider and your written verdict. Rules: [Grading & Revenue Ladder](../../resources/GRADING_RUBRIC.md).
