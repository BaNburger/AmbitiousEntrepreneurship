# Essential Reading List

Updated: 2026-10-20.

*9 pieces. That's it. Each one changes how you think about building.*

---

## Phase 1: Before You Start (Read in Week 1-2)

### 1. Paul Graham — "How to Get Startup Ideas"
**Link:** https://paulgraham.com/startideas.html
**Read time:** 25 min
**The insight:** The best startup ideas come from noticing problems you personally have, not from brainstorming. "Live in the future and build what's missing." Stop trying to think of startup ideas. Start noticing what's broken.
**Assign before:** Module 2 (Finding Ideas)

### 2. Paul Graham — "Do Things That Don't Scale"
**Link:** https://paulgraham.com/ds.html
**Read time:** 15 min
**The insight:** Every great startup started with founders doing things manually — recruiting users one by one, delivering the product by hand, writing personal emails. This feels wrong but it's how you learn. The Airbnb founders photographed apartments themselves. Stripe's founders installed their software on users' laptops in person.
**Assign before:** Module 8 (Finding First Users)

### 3. Rob Fitzpatrick — "The Mom Test" (Book)
**Link:** https://themomtest.com (buy the book — it's €15 and 130 pages)
**Read time:** 2-3 hours
**The insight:** People will lie to you about your startup idea because they're polite. This book teaches you how to ask questions that make it impossible for them to lie. The single most valuable skill for a first-time founder.
**Assign before:** Module 3 (The Problem Space)

## Phase 2: While Building (Read in Week 3-6)

### 4. Paul Graham — "Startup = Growth"
**Link:** https://paulgraham.com/growth.html
**Read time:** 20 min
**The insight:** A startup is not just a small company. A startup is a company designed for rapid growth. If you're not growing, you're a small business — and that's fine, but it changes every decision you make. Growth rate is the compass.
**Assign before:** Module 9 (Prototype to Product)

### 5. Rahul Vohra / First Round Review — "How Superhuman Built an Engine to Find Product-Market Fit"
**Link:** https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/
**Read time:** 30 min
**The insight:** The most concrete, reproducible process ever published for measuring and improving product-market fit. Based on Sean Ellis's "very disappointed" survey question. Gives you a number to steer by (the 40% line, read as a compass in [framework 06](../frameworks/06_PMF_Measurement_Engine.md)), the exact segmentation method, and the exact improvement loop.
**Assign before:** Module 10 (Product Management & Growth)

### 6. Andrej Karpathy — "Software 2.0"
**Link:** https://karpathy.medium.com/software-2-0-a64152b37c35
**Read time:** 15 min
**The insight:** Traditional software is written by programmers. Software 2.0 is written by optimizers (neural networks) on data. This reframes what it means to "build" an AI product — your moat is in the data and evaluation, not the code. Essential for understanding where AI startup value lives.
**Assign before:** Module 6 (Rapid Prototyping with AI)

## Phase 3: Scaling & Pitching (Read in Week 7-12)

### 7. Paul Graham — "How to Raise Money"
**Link:** https://paulgraham.com/fr.html
**Read time:** 20 min
**The insight:** Fundraising is a separate skill from building a company. It has its own rules, dynamics, and traps. The most common mistake: raising too early, from the wrong people, for the wrong amount. If you raise, be deliberate about it.
**Assign before:** Module 12 (Fundraising)

### 8. Sam Altman — "Startup Playbook"
**Link:** https://playbook.samaltman.com
**Read time:** 20 min
**The insight:** The compressed wisdom of YC in 5,000 words. Covers idea, team, product, execution, and growth in a way that's specific enough to be actionable. Good capstone reading that ties all modules together.
**Assign before:** Module 12 (Final Presentations)

### 9. YC Startup School — Free Video Course
**Link:** https://www.startupschool.org
**Time commitment:** Pick 3-4 lectures relevant to your current stage
**The insight:** 20+ hours of free lectures from YC partners who've guided thousands of startups. Not a "watch it all" resource — pick the lecture that matches your current chokepoint. The Michael Seibel lectures on product-market fit and the Dalton Caldwell lectures on evaluating ideas are particularly strong.
**Assign as:** Ongoing supplementary resource

## What's NOT on This List (and Why)

| Book/Resource | Why it's cut |
|--------------|-------------|
| "The Lean Startup" (Eric Ries) | The core ideas (Build-Measure-Learn) are already in the slides. The book is 300 pages of what PG says in 3 pages. Read the Wikipedia summary if curious. |
| "Sprint" (Jake Knapp) | Designed for design teams at Google with 5-day budgets. Student teams can't afford this level of structure. |
| "Zero to One" (Peter Thiel) | Philosophically interesting but not actionable for a 12-week course. |
| "Business Model Canvas" (Osterwalder) | The canvas is more useful for optimizing existing businesses than for discovering new ones. Use Lean Canvas if you need a visual. |
| Any growth hacking book | Premature optimization. Get 10 paying users first. |
| "Crossing the Chasm" (Geoffrey Moore) | Relevant at Series B, not at pre-seed. |

## How to Read

Don't read linearly. Read the piece assigned for your current module, then come back to it after running your experiments. The second reading always reveals something the first didn't, because now you have context from your own failures.

**Budget: 2-3 hours of reading per week. No more.** The rest of your time should be spent talking to customers and building.
