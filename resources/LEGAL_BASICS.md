# Just Enough to Keep You Out of Jail

**This is not legal advice.** We are not lawyers. This is the short list of things student teams actually trip over — the 20% that prevents 80% of the mess. When real money or real personal data starts flowing, buy one hour with a real lawyer (the startup legal office hours are free — book them).

## 1. Recording interviews

Ask BEFORE you hit record. One sentence is enough:

> *"Ich würde das Gespräch gerne aufnehmen, nur für unsere interne Auswertung — ist das okay?"* / *"I'd like to record this for our internal notes only — is that okay?"*

Get a yes on the recording. Store recordings privately (team drive, not public), don't share clips outside the team, delete if someone asks. That's it.

## 2. Your landing page

You're collecting emails in Germany. Three things, thirty minutes:

- **Impressum** — legally required on German websites, even test pages. Use a generator (e.g. e-recht24), paste, done.
- **Privacy line** near the form: *"We store your email only to contact you about [product]. No sharing, unsubscribe anytime, contact: [email]."* If your form tool offers double opt-in, turn it on.
- **Don't** buy email lists, scrape addresses, or import your interviewees into a mailing tool without asking them.

Analytics: prefer a cookieless EU tool (e.g. Plausible) and you can skip the cookie banner circus entirely.

## 3. Taking money

Covered properly in Module 09. The survival version: an invoice needs 7 fields (both names & addresses, date, invoice number, description, amount, your §19 no-VAT note if you're under the Kleinunternehmer limits). A team taking money together with no setup is a **GbR — you are all personally liable**. Fine for pilots; revisit when real risk shows up.

## 4. The email list after the course

The consents you collected are for THIS product. If you kill the idea: delete the list. If you pivot: ask again ("we've changed direction — still want updates?"). Don't carry lists into your next project like furniture.

## 5. Other people's stuff

Don't paste competitor screenshots into your product, don't use unlicensed images/fonts on your landing page (use Unsplash/Pexels-style licensed sources), don't copy someone's pricing page word-for-word. Code from your own laptop is yours; code built on university lab infrastructure or in a paid job — check first (TUM ForTe / Venture Labs answer this for free).

## When to actually get help

Any of these = book the (free) legal office hour BEFORE proceeding: handling health or financial data · customers demanding a signed contract you don't understand · first employee or intern · incorporation (UG/GmbH) · anyone threatening you with anything.
