# Zoho: Where to Sell Software Built in Chennai

In 1996, Sridhar Vembu had to decide where to look for customers for a software business whose code would be written in India.[^1] [^2]

## The founders

Vembu recalled in 2007 that he had studied at the Indian Institute of Technology in Madras, had come to Princeton in 1989 for a PhD and had joined Qualcomm in San Diego in 1994. "My PhD is in electrical engineering, so I really do not have a software background," he said.[^1] His brother "was also there at Qualcomm as a software engineer" and "wanted to return home to India". Vembu said that "Software is a great business to start in India".[^1] In 2011 he recalled that in 1995 he and his brother Kumar "constantly spoke about what we could do with our talent, about how we could make a difference in the lives of people back home in Chennai". He added, "We wanted to make money, but more importantly we were passionate about making a difference."[^2]

Their partner was Tony Thomas, who, in Vembu's words, "had some network management software and was experienced in the area".[^1] According to the company, the founders started writing code in Java, "an emerging platform at the time", in 1996. It said its first focus was network management software for businesses in the telecom industry.[^3]

## Two places to sell

The sources do not say which options the founders weighed. Exhibit 1 compares India, where the software was to be written, with the United States, where Vembu had studied and worked. In 1996 India had 979.7 million people, a GDP per head of USD 401 and 0.046 internet users per 100 people, which is about 450,000 users. The United States had 269.4 million people, a GDP per head of USD 29,968 and 16.4 internet users per 100 people, which is about 44 million users (Exhibit 1).[^4] India had 3.6 times the people of the United States, and the United States had about 75 times India's GDP per head.

Vembu's brother wanted to return to India, and Vembu saw software as a good business to start there. Vembu had to decide whether the first customers would be found in India or in the United States.
