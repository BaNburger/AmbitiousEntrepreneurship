# Klarna: Which Markets Come After the Nordics

In July 2009, Sebastian Siemiatkowski, 27, chief executive and co-founder of the Stockholm company Kreditor, had to decide which countries outside the Nordics the company would enter next.[^1] Kreditor let people shop online and pay by invoice after delivery. On 29 June it had announced that it would change its name to Klarna in the autumn.[^2] Klarna's own history later explained that "a new name is needed that can be marketed outside the Nordic countries".[^3] The sources do not date the decision about the next countries.

## Sweden and the Nordics

Three students at the Stockholm School of Economics, Siemiatkowski, Victor Jacobsson and Niklas Adalberth, started Kreditor in 2005. Shops that connected to it could offer payment after delivery. Siemiatkowski explained in 2009 that shops needed their money at once and that Kreditor stepped in as the intermediary.[^1] According to the company, Kreditor opened its payment solution to consumers in Norway, Denmark and Finland in 2008.[^3] Siemiatkowski recalled in 2024 that the first markets after Sweden, which he named as Norway and Finland, were "partially driven by merchant demand", because "a lot of Swedish ecommerce merchants were active" there.[^4]

In July 2009, Dagens Nyheter reported that Kreditor had about 4,000 online shops connected, more than one million consumers using it and about 120 employees. Its annualised revenue was about SEK 200 million. It handled payments of SEK 2 billion to 3 billion a year and the newspaper reported that Kreditor had been profitable since it began in 2005.[^1] The Swedish financial regulator had recently authorised Kreditor as a credit market company, which, the newspaper wrote, gave it room to broaden its business, among other things by taking deposits from customers.[^1] The press release on the new name called it part of a brand strategy to raise sales for the 4,000 connected shops, and it said that more than 1.2 million consumers had paid through one of Kreditor's two solutions, invoice or instalments.[^2]

## Markets beyond the Nordics

Siemiatkowski described the choice in 2024 in terms of how people paid. In Germany and the Netherlands there was "a strong tradition of what in Germany is referred to as Rechnung, which is basically invoice or buy-now-pay-later". In the United States and the United Kingdom, he said, "credit cards are so prevalent".[^4] The four markets he named differed greatly in size (Exhibit 1).[^5]

By July 2009 Kreditor served four Nordic markets, was profitable and had announced a name it could market outside the Nordics. Siemiatkowski had to decide which countries to enter first, those where people already paid on invoice or those where most paid by card.
